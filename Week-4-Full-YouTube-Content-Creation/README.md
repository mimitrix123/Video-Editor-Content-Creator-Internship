## Video 1 Script

### 00:00–00:15 — Hook

**Visual:** Fast montage of raw footage versus finished footage, punch-ins, captions, sound effects, and transitions.

**Voiceover:**

> Your footage does not need to be expensive to look professional. In this video, I’ll show you five simple editing techniques that can make your videos cleaner, faster, and much more engaging.

### 00:15–00:30 — Intro

> I’m going to cover pacing, jump cuts, B-roll, sound design, and text animation. You can apply all five techniques in almost any editing software.

### 00:30–01:15 — Technique 1: Better Pacing

> Start by removing pauses, repeated words, and unnecessary gaps. A tighter edit keeps attention moving forward. Cut whenever the story or idea has finished its job.

**On-screen text:** `1. CUT THE DEAD SPACE`

### 01:15–02:00 — Technique 2: Intentional Jump Cuts

> Jump cuts work best when they have a purpose. Instead of cutting randomly, use them to remove time or move quickly between ideas. Change framing slightly with a punch-in so the cut feels intentional.

**On-screen text:** `2. USE PURPOSEFUL JUMP CUTS`

### 02:00–02:50 — Technique 3: B-Roll

> B-roll gives the viewer something relevant to look at while you continue the explanation. Match the footage to the words being spoken, and cut it on natural beats in the narration or music.

**On-screen text:** `3. ADD RELEVANT B-ROLL`

### 02:50–03:40 — Technique 4: Sound Design

> Small sound effects can make edits feel much more polished. Use subtle whooshes, clicks, impacts, room tone, and transitions. Keep them underneath the voice instead of competing with it.

**On-screen text:** `4. DESIGN THE SOUND`

### 03:40–04:30 — Technique 5: Text & Motion

> Use text to reinforce important information, not to repeat every word. Animate titles quickly and keep them inside mobile-safe areas. Consistency is more important than complicated effects.

**On-screen text:** `5. KEEP TEXT SIMPLE`

### 04:30–04:50 — Recap

> So remember: tighten the pacing, make jump cuts intentional, use B-roll, design the sound, and keep your text simple.

### 04:50–05:00 — CTA / Outro

> If this helped, subscribe for more practical editing tips, and check out the next tutorial for a complete editing workflow.

**End screen:** Subscribe button + Video 2 recommendation.

## Video 2 Script

### 00:00–00:15 — Hook

> Here’s how I would take a folder of raw clips and turn it into a polished YouTube video—from organising the footage to the final export.

### 00:15–00:40 — Organise

> First, separate footage into A-roll, B-roll, audio, graphics, music, and exports. Good organisation saves time throughout the entire edit.

### 00:40–01:20 — Rough Cut

> Build the story using the strongest takes first. Remove mistakes and dead space before adding effects. The goal is to make the story work even without music or graphics.

### 01:20–02:00 — B-Roll & Pacing

> Cover cuts with relevant B-roll and vary shot sizes. Use punch-ins and occasional speed changes only where they improve the rhythm.

### 02:00–02:45 — Audio

> Clean dialogue first, then balance music and effects underneath it. Listen on headphones and speakers so the voice remains easy to understand.

### 02:45–03:30 — Color

> Correct exposure and white balance before applying a creative look. Keep skin tones natural and make shots from different cameras feel consistent.

### 03:30–04:15 — Graphics

> Add titles, call-outs, captions, and a consistent lower-third style. Keep animations short and readable.

### 04:15–04:45 — Final Review

> Watch the complete video without editing. Check pacing, audio peaks, spelling, graphics, transitions, and continuity.

### 04:45–05:00 — CTA

> Export the final version, upload it with a strong title and thumbnail, and use the description to help viewers understand exactly what they’ll learn.
